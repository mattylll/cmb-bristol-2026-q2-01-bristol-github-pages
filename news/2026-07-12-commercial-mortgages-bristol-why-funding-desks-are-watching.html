Commercial Mortgages Bristol: Why Funding Desks Are Watching the 118 Home Iron Acton Way Consent in Yate

Nobody gets excited about a landscape maintenance plan. But when one lands on a planning register, it usually means a developer is clearing the runway. That is exactly what appears to be happening in Yate, where application DOC26/00253 sits pending decision on the South Gloucestershire Council planning register (Idox).

The paperwork relates to Land North Of Iron Acton Way And East Of Dyer’s Lane, Yate, South Gloucestershire. According to the South Gloucestershire Council planning register (Idox), the submission asks the council to discharge three conditions on Planning Permission P19/2575/F: condition 21 (Tree Method Statement), condition 22 (Landscape Implementation) and condition 23 (Landscape Maintenance). The parent consent, granted through the Full Planning route, permits 118 dwellings alongside public open space, drainage, landscaping and ancillary works. The register lists the use class as residential and confirms the 118 unit count.

Why the timing matters more than the trees

In our experience, tree and landscape conditions sit near the back of the pre-commencement queue. When a sponsor starts clearing them, groundworks are usually not far behind. Funders read these filings the same way we do: consent plus condition discharge equals a project about to start drawing cash. For anyone financing, bidding on, or selling into the north Bristol fringe, this filing is the tell.

Sizing the cheque

Construction Capital puts the gross development value at £38,951,800, working from figures drawn from the South Gloucestershire Council planning register (Idox). Around £39 million is classic mid-market Bristol territory: too big for casual funding, comfortably deliverable by one sponsor with the right facilities in place. Current criteria, product detail and local worked examples for schemes like this sit on the Bristol page of our parent site, covering development, bridging and investment terms across the city and its districts.

A residential build of 118 units typically leans on three funding layers over its life:

  1. Senior development debt. On a scheme this size, specialist commercial lenders and challenger banks will usually advance 60 to 70 percent of GDV, released in stages against monitoring surveyor sign-off.
  2. Mezzanine or stretch senior. Sponsors wanting less equity in the deal can layer stretch facilities on top, for a price. At roughly £39 million GDV, moving from 60 to 70 percent leverage frees up around £3.9 million of sponsor cash.
  3. Exit finance. Phased sellers often refinance completed units onto development exit products from bridging specialists, trimming the cost of the senior facility while sales run off. Sponsors keeping units should be arranging investment mortgages or portfolio terms well ahead of practical completion.

What we would tell the sponsor

Get terms agreed before the contractor mobilises. Appoint the monitoring surveyor before the first drawdown, not during it. Price the exit bridge while the scheme is still in conditions, because that is when the numbers are calm. Watch DOC26/00253: schemes at this stage set the borrowing demand our desk sees over the following year, and the smooth fundings are always the ones arranged early.
